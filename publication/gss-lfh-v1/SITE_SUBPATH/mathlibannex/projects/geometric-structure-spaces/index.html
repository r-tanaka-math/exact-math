From unit-ball geometry to C*-algebras

The geometric structure space connects faces and supporting functionals of the norm unit ball with irreducible representations and primitive ideals. The project follows those connections to normal parts, compact representations, decomposition and rigidity.

Read the mathematical overview · Research project · Reader Project PDF

Assumptions and limits

The geometric construction begins with real or complex normed spaces; the C*-algebra comparisons retain their complex, possibly nonunital hypotheses. The zero space has empty geometric structure space. Individual results retain their nonzero, closedness, irreducibility and continuity assumptions.

  • For the elementary-model rigidity result, at least one algebra is *-isomorphic to the whole K(H) of a complex Hilbert space.
  • For finite-dimensional rigidity, at least one algebra is finite-dimensional over the complex numbers.
  • For bounded-operator rigidity, at least one algebra is *-isomorphic to the whole B(H) of a complex Hilbert space.

These conclusions equate the existence of a *-isomorphism with the existence of a GSS homeomorphism. They do not assert that a specified nonlinear homeomorphism is induced by that isomorphism. The exact manuscripts govern zero-algebra and zero-model cases. An abstract type-I factor is not substituted for the concrete B(H) model assumption.

GSS rigidity when either algebra has an elementary model · GSS rigidity when either algebra is finite-dimensional · GSS rigidity from a full bounded-operator model

Mathematical routes

These six groups follow the accepted reading guide. Choosing a group includes its prerequisite closure. The displayed levels always retain the full selected scope.

Closure spaces and norm geometry

Begin with hull–kernel closure on the kernel families obtained from faces. Closure need not be topological; finite continuity tests finite subsets, whereas continuity tests every subset.

Faces, pure states and spectra

Faces and extreme dual functionals lead to pure states and representation components. Unitary-equivalence classes of irreducible representations and their kernels remain different objects; the two spectral comparisons use their respective hull–kernel topologies.

Normal parts and semilinear structure

Pass from a representation component to its coefficient-kernel normal part with relative closure. Projective parametrization is a bijection. The same map, its inverse, the scalar automorphism and global orientation retain the hypotheses specified in each manuscript; full continuity requires extra conditions.

The central characterization

The faithful, fully normal representation condition and continuous finite conjugation meet in the elementary-algebra characterization. Retain the same GNS model, equality of the compact-operator image, singleton condition, and two-sided conjugate continuity.

From components to classification

Use compact comparisons to read representation-theoretic conditions. Compact containment differs from equality; prescribed dimension differs from an upper bound. Transport through a specified homeomorphism retains that specified map.

Compact representations, decomposition and rigidity

Faithful compact representation leads to the canonical c₀-decomposition indexed by minimal nonzero closed ideals. Its compact-operator summands are distinct from compact operators on a Hilbert direct sum. The final rigidity results assert existence of an isomorphism, with the different model hypotheses below.
